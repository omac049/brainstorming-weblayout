Key Takeaways for UAGC
Form Weight
UAGC's 2-step form is mid-pack. Empire State: 4 fields. SNHU: 14. CSU Global and UT Tyler: 5–6 required. No competitor uses a two-step wizard like UAGC — WGU's 3-step is the exception, on a separate subdomain.
Form-in-Hero
Only UT Tyler embeds the RFI directly in the hero (Slate). SNHU and WGU link out. CSU Global just below fold. Empire State sidebar. UAGC's on-page approach is not unusual but the 3.23x rage-click rate suggests execution problems, not strategy problems.
Mobile CTA
No competitor has a sticky bottom CTA bar consuming 45% of mobile viewport. CSU Global persists header CTAs; UT Tyler desktop-only. This is a UAGC-specific friction pattern.
Chat
4 of 5 competitors have visible chatbots. UAGC has none on audited pages — a notable gap.
Personalization
None show dynamic first-visit personalization. Empire State's "What Brings You Here?" tiles and UT Tyler's form triage are the strongest signals. SNHU carries browsing context into the RFI form. UAGC lacks audience triage or context carry-through.

What Works

Personalization + Next-Step CTA

The personalized thank-you URL (with sid, submissionID, and token parameters) injects the user's first name into the hero heading — "Congratulations [FirstName], you've taken the first step." The "Start Application" button links directly to the admission portal, creating a clear next-step path from RFI to application. This is the correct funnel progression: RFI → Confirmation → Application Portal.

Note: The base URL (no params) shows "Congratulations ," with a trailing comma — this is the fallback state when accessed without a valid session token.

Critical Issues

Critical

1. RFI Form Still Visible After Submission

The full RFI form appears in at least three places on the thank-you page: the left sidebar, a sticky/modal overlay, and the "Let us help" chat form. A user who just submitted their information sees the exact same form asking them to submit again.

Impact: Creates anxiety ("did my submission work?") and risks duplicate lead creation if resubmitted.

Critical

2. No Submission Confirmation Details

No confirmation number, no email confirmation notice, no recap of what was submitted (program of interest, contact info), no estimated response time. The user has no tangible proof their request went through beyond the "Congratulations" heading.

Impact: Missed trust signal. Users expect a receipt — especially when providing phone/email for sales contact.

Critical

3. Start Date Module Is Empty

The "Starting Soon!" module renders "Start Date" as a label with no actual date. Either a data feed issue or the component fails to populate without a specific program context.

Impact: Urgency signal is completely lost. An empty date widget looks broken.

Major Issues

Major

4. Page Length Is Excessive

13+ content modules requiring 15+ scroll depths. Best practice for post-conversion: focused confirmation with 2–3 clear next steps — not a mini-site.

Major

5. "Request Info" CTA Still in Header

The global header's "Request Info" button remains active and visible. On a thank-you page, this CTA is obsolete and competes with "Start Application."

Major

6. Stale Blog Content

All four article cards date from Dec 2021 – Dec 2022. Pre-decision content ("7 Reasons to Go Back to School") is not post-submission relevant, and it's 3–4 years old.

Major

7. Competing CTAs Dilute Conversion

At least 5 distinct CTAs: Start Application, Program Information, Apply Now, Apply for Free, Learn More (×4). "Start Application" and "Apply Now" appear to go to different destinations.
