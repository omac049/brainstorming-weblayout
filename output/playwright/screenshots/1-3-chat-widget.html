Mobile viewport analysis of fixed/sticky elements — sticky RFI bar, tracking footer, and chat affordances — across 3 device sizes and 10 representative pages.

Executive Summary

No Traditional Chat Widget — But a Critical Sticky Bar Problem

  • No HubSpot, Drift, Intercom, or LiveChat widget detected on any page. Automated detection scanned 16 chat-specific selectors across all 10 pages and 3 viewports. The only "chat" elements found are static chatImage icons embedded in the organic site footer (not a floating widget).
  • The stickyrfi-mobile bar is the dominant fixed element — present on ALL pages, ALL viewports. It's a full-width, 72px-tall sticky CTA bar fixed to the bottom of every mobile viewport at z-index 98.
  • Homepage has a double-stacking problem: a 232px tracking-footer-block (z-index 99) sits ON TOP of the sticky RFI bar, consuming up to 45.6% of the iPhone SE viewport on first load.
  • On iPhone SE (375×667), the sticky RFI bar alone consumes 10.8% of the visible viewport — 72px of 667px. Content area shrinks to ~595px effective height.

Key Findings

1
Homepage Tracking Footer Steals 45% of Mobile Viewport

The tracking-footer-block (232px, z-99) stacks on top of the sticky RFI bar (72px, z-98) on the homepage. On iPhone SE, this leaves only 363px for content — less than the height of a typical hero image. This likely contributes to the 11.7% Contentsquare scroll depth: users may not perceive there's more content below.

2
Sticky RFI Bar Competes with Hero RFI Form

On paid landing pages, the hero section already contains an RFI form (FORM-01). The sticky RFI bar (FORM-05) appears simultaneously, creating two competing CTAs in the same viewport. Combined with the 3.23× form rage-click data from Contentsquare, this dual-form pattern may be confusing users about where to interact.

3
No Chat Widget — Potential Support Gap

No floating chat widget (HubSpot, Drift, Intercom, LiveChat) was detected on any page. The only chat-related element is a static #chatImage (32×32px) embedded in the organic site footer — not visible until users scroll to the very bottom. For a site with 47.9% mobile traffic and 1.07M monthly pageviews, this may represent a missed live-support opportunity, especially on high-intent pages like tuition/aid and application funnel.

4
Sticky RFI Bar Height Is Consistent but Not Responsive

The 72px bar height is identical across all three viewports. On the smallest device (375×667), it consumes 10.8%; on the largest (414×896), 8.0%. A responsive approach — reducing bar height to ~56px on small screens — would recover 16px of content area, improving the above-fold experience for the ~15% of mobile users on SE-class devices.
