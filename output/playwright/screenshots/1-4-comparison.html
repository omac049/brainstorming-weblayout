Contentsquare UX Layer — Paid Success Pages

All 10 paid pages share the same CS "Paid Success" page group. Key behavioral signals: 23.2% scroll depth (users rarely reach below-fold content), 12.2% activity rate (low engagement despite 89.2% form exposure), and 6.70s average load time (nearly 2× the blog's 4.12s). The two-step RFI form shows 3.23× click recurrence with only 0.04% conversion per click — the single largest frustration signal site-wide. This form friction is shared across all 10 pages, meaning the tier differences above reflect content/structure quality around a universally struggling form.

Synthesis
Key Findings & Recommendations

Executive takeaways

Contentsquare Adds a Behavioral Layer

The structural comparison above uses GA4 metrics (engagement, bounce, sessions). Contentsquare zoning data reveals that while page structure explains the tier spread, the form itself is the universal bottleneck. The two-step RFI form's 3.23× click recurrence and 0.04% conversion per click is identical across all 10 paid pages regardless of tier — meaning even Tier 1 pages (v7, courses v5) are leaving conversions on the table due to form friction, not content gaps.

  • The form isn’t the problem — content surrounding it is. Every high-traffic page repeats FORM-01 / FORM-05 (and many add FORM-02); divergence in performance maps to whether visitors get program context, not to the field layout itself.
  • Program discovery (PROG-01 + PROG-02) is the strongest predictor of conversion among these ten URLs. The two >7% pages both pair category grids with deep lists; mid-tier pages typically drop the grid or bury the list.
  • Channel mix matters. Paid Social–first destinations (especially P1 and the P8–P10 program LPs) show higher bounce in this dataset regardless of duplicated forms — calibration of expectations and landing strategy per channel is required before blaming “RFI fatigue.”
  • The three program-specific LPs (P8–P10) share a template but underperform because Paid Social traffic doesn’t match their intent level. Cold prospects need career outcomes, proof, and browse paths above the fold — not only a vertical headline and RFI.
