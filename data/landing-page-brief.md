# Agent Brief: Optimize “Discover Online School Done Right | UAGC” Landing Page

## Source Context
This brief is based on the uploaded content brief for the UAGC landing page “Discover Online School Done Right.” The source simulation tested 30 personas across alumni, competitor, faculty/staff, influencer, parent/family, and prospective student segments.

## Executive Summary
The page has a strong foundation. The message “Online School Done Right,” flexible scheduling, one-class-at-a-time pacing, and the 3-week risk-free trial resonated with multiple audience segments. However, the page does not yet fully overcome skepticism around online education quality, UAGC reputation, cost transparency, transferability, employer recognition, and career outcomes.

The rewrite should keep the current emotional hook but add stronger evidence. The page needs to feel less like a marketing landing page and more like a transparent decision guide for cautious prospective students and their families.

## Primary Goal
Create or optimize a landing page that increases trust, lowers perceived risk, and motivates prospective students to request information or apply by clearly explaining:

- Why UAGC is credible
- How online learning works
- What support students receive
- What it costs
- How credits transfer
- What outcomes students can expect
- Why the 3-week risk-free trial reduces risk

## Target Page

## What to Keep and Amplify
The following messages worked and should remain prominent:

1. **“Online School Done Right”**
   - Strong, memorable positioning.
   - Feels direct and reassuring.
   - Should remain close to the top of the page.

2. **Flexible scheduling**
   - Important for working adults, parents, military-affiliated students, and returning learners.
   - Should be explained with concrete examples.

3. **3-week risk-free trial**
   - One of the strongest conversion-supporting messages.
   - Should be positioned as a practical way to test online learning before committing.

4. **One class at a time**
   - Makes the experience feel manageable.
   - Especially useful for anxious learners, parents, and adults returning to school.

5. **University of Arizona association**
   - Builds immediate recognition.
   - Needs clarification so users understand the relationship between UAGC and the University of Arizona.

## What Must Improve
The biggest opportunity is to replace vague reassurance with concrete proof.

### Trust and Reputation Gaps
Users want:

- Clear WSCUC accreditation details
- Employer recognition evidence
- Career outcome data
- Graduate employment or advancement statistics
- Alumni network information
- Explanation of how UAGC relates to the University of Arizona
- Evidence that online learning is rigorous and legitimate

### Cost and Aid Gaps
Users want:

- Actual per-credit costs
- Financial aid options
- Scholarship details
- Military and GI Bill benefit guidance
- Payment plan information
- Cost-saving pathways through transfer credits or prior learning
- Comparison context against community college or other online options

### Transfer and Credit Gaps
Users want:

- How credits transfer into UAGC
- Whether old credits, including credits from 25+ years ago, may count
- How credits transfer to ASU, NAU, or other Arizona public universities
- Whether concurrent credit is “real credit”
- How prior learning assessment works

### Program Discovery Gaps
Users want more program detail before filling out a form, especially around:

- General Studies
- Liberal Arts
- Business
- Leadership
- Executive leadership
- Specific curriculum structures
- Career paths tied to programs

### CTA and Form Friction
The “Request More Information” form may feel pushy if it asks for a phone number too early. Keep the CTA, but add reassuring microcopy:

> “It only takes a minute. No obligation.”

Also consider adding a softer secondary CTA:

> “Explore Programs” or “See Tuition and Aid Options”

## Objection Handling Map

| Objection | Page Response |
|---|---|
| Online feels less legitimate | Explain accreditation, rigor, faculty, support, and employer relevance |
| Fear of predatory online schools | Lead with transparency, WSCUC accreditation, cost clarity, and no-obligation trial |
| Too expensive vs. community college | Show actual costs, transfer savings, aid, scholarships, and value context |
| Lack of employer recognition | Add outcomes, career services, employer recognition proof, and alumni examples |
| Parents want a name-brand school | Clarify UAGC relationship with University of Arizona and accreditation |
| Old credits may not transfer | Explain transfer review and prior learning options |
| Online technology may be hard | Highlight orientation, tech support, and student support services |
| Request form feels pushy | Add no-obligation microcopy and softer secondary CTA |
| Programs are unclear | Add program cards or links before the form |

### Tone
Use a reassuring, transparent, practical tone. Avoid hype. The audience is interested but skeptical, so the copy should sound credible, specific, and helpful.

### Do Not Do

- Do not overpromise career or salary outcomes.
- Do not claim employer recognition without proof.
- Do not imply University of Arizona main-campus equivalence unless approved language supports it.
- Do not hide cost information behind vague “affordable” language.
- Do not make the form feel like the only way to get basic information.
- Do not bury accreditation.

## Summary for the Agent
The page should not just say UAGC is flexible. It should prove UAGC is credible, transparent, supportive, and worth considering. Keep the strong “Online School Done Right” framing, but build the page around the skeptical user’s real questions: cost, credit transfer, accreditation, outcomes, support, rigor, and whether the decision is safe.
