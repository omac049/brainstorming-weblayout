1
The Data Says Fix the Funnel
~1 min
💥

"We've been talking about this as a traffic problem. It's not. We have 11.1 million sessions and $8.9 million in ad spend driving people to the site. The traffic is there."

🗣

"The problem is what happens when they arrive. Over 627,000 paid visitors bounced from our primary landing page last year. They came, they saw the page, and they left."

🗣

"What I'm going to show you is 12 months of data from GSC, GA4, Contentsquare, and Google Ads — and a clear, phased plan to fix it."

💡

Confident, not alarmist. Opportunity framing, not blame. Don't linger — this slide sets up the punchline on slide 2.

➡

"Let me show you the single most important finding in the data."

2
4.5x Conversion Gap
~1.5 min
🎯

Pause 2–3 seconds after clicking. Let the room read "4.5x" before you speak.

🗣

"Our primary paid landing page — the one that gets 1.18 million sessions — converts at 3%. Another page on the same site, with similar form elements, converts at 13.6%. That's a 4.5x gap between our own pages."

💥

"Same school. Similar form elements. The difference is the content layout — what visitors see and do before they ever reach a form."

Blog tease
🗣

"And it's not just paid pages. We have a blog post about PhD programs that gets 149,000 engaged readers a year — people spending 8 minutes reading. It produces 21 conversions. Twenty-one. Because there's no CTA on the page."

No — and we're not targeting that for every page. But if we can move the primary RFI page from 3% to even 7% (half the best page's rate), that's still a doubling. I'll show you the math on slide 9.

➡

"Let me give you a quick sense of the scale we're working with."

3
The Scale of the Opportunity
~2 min
Top-line numbers (left side of slide)
🗣

"11.1 million sessions, 1.73 million organic clicks, 348,000 conversions. This isn't a small site."

🎯

Point to the engagement rate metric.

💥

"But our engagement rate is only 47.5%. More than half of all visitors leave without meaningfully interacting. And 60% of our traffic is mobile — where engagement is 6 points lower than desktop. That gap represents hundreds of thousands of failed sessions."

Channel insight (right side of slide)
🗣

"Quick channel view: Paid Search is our top converter — 77,500 conversions, 61% engagement. Display overperforms at 59,600 conversions and 64% engagement."

💥

"But Paid Social brings 1.91 million sessions — our largest paid channel — at only 42% engagement and 58% bounce. That traffic is landing on pages that aren't built for it."

Homepage organic clicks are down 41.6% half-over-half. Blog content down 49.7%. Likely AI overview cannibalization. This makes conversion efficiency even more urgent — fewer free visits means each one has to work harder.

➡

"Let me show you exactly what I mean. Here are two of our pages side by side."

4
Tale of Two Pages
~2 min
🎯

Point to the LEFT screenshot as you describe the bad page.

🗣

"This is our primary RFI landing page. 1.18 million sessions, mostly Paid Social. 53% bounce. 3% conversion. Look at what greets visitors: a generic form with generic copy. No program specifics. No reason to believe."

🎯

Point to the RIGHT screenshot.

🗣

"This is degree-programs-v7. 222,000 sessions, mostly Display. 25% bounce. 13.57% conversion. What's different? The layout. This page leads with a program category browser. Visitors explore degrees, see salary data, read outcomes — then they reach the form already informed and motivated."

💥

"The forms are similar. The layout is completely different. One page earns attention before it asks for information. The other asks first and earns nothing."

💡

If you have time, add the visitor perspective: "You click a social ad about education. You land on a page that immediately asks for your info with no context. 53% of people leave."

Traffic source matters, but it doesn't explain a 4.5x gap. Paid Search visitors (highest intent) still convert at only 3.5% on the bachelor's page. Content-rich pages outperform regardless of source. Organic shows the same: /online-degrees has 88.8% engagement vs. homepage at 56.7%.

➡

"That's two pages. Let me show you it's not an anomaly — it's a pattern across all five paid landing pages."

5
It's a System-Wide Gap
~1.5 min
🎯

Walk through the table top to bottom. Let the green highlights on the bottom two rows do the work.

🗣

"Here are all five paid landing pages. The top three — request-info, bachelor's, military — have the most traffic. Engagement rates in the 40s and 50s. Conversion rates around 3%."

🎯

Point to the bottom two rows — courses-v5 and degree-programs-v7.

💥

"Now look at the bottom two. Fewer sessions — but 75% engagement, 7 to 13% conversion. These two pages, with less traffic, produce nearly as many or more conversions than the top three combined."

💥

"Better pages beat bigger traffic. If request-info-v5 converted at even 7%, that's 47,000 additional leads from existing traffic. No new ad spend required."

Intent is part of it, but both /request-info and /degree-programs receive paid traffic from similar campaigns. The engagement rates tell the story: 74% vs. 46%. Visitors on the content-rich pages stay, explore, and convert. Visitors on the generic pages bounce.

➡

"It's not just paid pages where we're leaving money on the table. Let me show you what's happening with our blog content."

6
149K Readers. 21 Conversions.
~1.5 min
💥

"We have a blog post about PhD vs. doctorate degrees. It ranks number 3 on Google. 149,000 people read it last year. They spend 8 minutes on the page. 68% engagement. And it produced 21 leads. Twenty-one."

🗣

"Why? Because the page doesn't tell them we're a university. There's no link to our doctoral program. No CTA. Nothing. They read about PhDs for 8 minutes and then leave."

🎯

Point to the 2,437 number on the right side of the slide.

🗣

"Across just three blog posts: 243,000 sessions, 84 total conversions. If we add one contextual CTA per post and get to just 1%, that's 2,437 leads per year. A 29x improvement from content that already exists."

💥

"This is the lowest-effort, highest-upside item on the list. The content already ranks. Already engages. We just need to add one link."

Informational traffic converts lower than branded, yes. But 0.01% isn't a floor — it's the absence of any conversion path. Even 0.5% gets us 1,200 leads. The current 21 is a rounding error.

➡

"And it's getting more urgent. Let me show you what's happening with organic."

7
Organic Is Declining
~1 min
🎯

Point to the three red stat cards across the top — let the big red numbers land before you speak. Then gesture to the homepage screenshot on the right.

🗣

"In six months, homepage organic clicks dropped 42%. Blog content — nearly 50%. Writing center, 32%. This is the homepage right here — the front door of the site — losing almost half its organic visitors."

💥

"This is AI overview cannibalization. Google is answering 'what is a PhD' and 'how to write a business plan' before anyone clicks. The site needs to offer what AI overviews can't."

🎯

Point to the amber callout box on the right: "Why this matters now."

💥

"If organic traffic is shrinking, conversion efficiency goes from 'nice to have' to survival mode. Every visit is more valuable now."

The login→myuagc migration is a portal change that affects existing students. It doesn't explain the blog or writing center drops, and those pages serve prospective students. The pattern is consistent with AI overview cannibalization across informational queries.

➡

"So with that context, here's what the ad efficiency data tells us about what works."

8
Audience-Specific Content Converts 18x Better
~1.5 min
💡

This is a "what works" slide, not a budget review. The two big stat cards ($29 vs $1,539) do the heavy lifting. The screenshots on the right show WHY. Stay on the page/content angle.

The $29 vs. $1,539 cards (left side)
🎯

Point to the green $29 card, then the red $1,539 card. Let the visual gap land before you speak.

🗣

"Military Display: $29 per lead, 8,752 conversions. PMax General: $1,539 per lead, 477 conversions. Same school. 18x cost difference."

The screenshots (right side)
🎯

Gesture to the two screenshots on the right. Top = generic RFI page. Bottom = content-rich page.

🗣

"Here's why. The top screenshot is where most ad traffic lands — a generic form page. The bottom is what an audience-specific page looks like: program browser, salary data, outcomes. Visitors explore, self-select, then convert."

💥

"The difference isn't the media buy. It's the landing page. Better pages make every ad dollar work harder. That's a website fix, not a media fix."

The media team controls the targeting and spend. We control the landing experience. Right now, even well-targeted campaigns underperform because the page doesn't match the audience. Fixing the page is the fastest lever we can pull — and it makes every campaign more efficient.

➡

"This isn't just theory. Other schools have done exactly this and the results are dramatic."

10
Where to Focus
~2 min
💥

P1: "Fix the primary RFI page. 1.18M sessions, 53% bounce. Enrich with program-specific content. If it matched our best page, that's +124K leads/year."

💥

P2: "Add CTAs to top blog content. PhD, business plan. One module per post. The content already works — we just need to connect it to our programs."

🗣

P3: "Mobile-first above the fold. 60% of traffic, underperforms everywhere. Chat widget, form placement, viewport."

🗣

P4: "Replicate winning layouts from /online-degrees and /degree-programs-v7 across all underperformers."

🗣

P5: "Strategic review of declining organic and PMax efficiency — longer-term but need attention."

💡

Note: P1 and P2 are quick wins before the full reskin. They're also the test cases — if enriching the RFI page works, it validates the full redesign approach.

➡

"Let me put a number on what this means."

11
+124K Additional Leads Per Year
~1.5 min
🎯

Pause when slide appears. Let the room absorb "+124K" in big green text. 3 seconds of silence.

🗣

"If our primary RFI page converted at the rate our best page already achieves — 13.57% — that's 159,754 conversions instead of 35,498. Same traffic. Same budget. Same ads."

💥

"Now, I want to be honest — 13.57% may not be realistic for the generic RFI page. That's the ceiling. But even halfway there — 3% to 7% — doubles our conversions. That's 47,000 additional leads with zero incremental ad spend."

💥

"The gap between where we are and where we could be is enormous. And the lever is the page experience, not the budget."

Acknowledge it's a theoretical max. Pivot: "Our working target is 7% conversion and sub-40% bounce. We'll validate with A/B testing in Phase 2 before committing to the full reskin."

➡

"Here's how we get there."

12
Next Steps — Three Phases
~2 min
🗣

Phase 1 — May: "Audit and document. Screenshot every key page, side-by-side analysis of why winning pages work, chat widget and mobile UX audit. This gives us the complete picture."

🗣

Phase 2 — June: "Quick wins. Enrich the RFI page, add blog CTAs, launch an A/B test. Changes we can make before a redesign — and they give us real data on whether the approach works."

🗣

Phase 3 — July: "Compile everything into a build-ready requirements doc. Present Phase 2 results. Go/no-go on the full reskin based on test data, not assumptions."

🎯

Point to the success metrics row at the bottom of the slide.

🗣

"We're measuring four things: RFI conversion from 3% to 7-plus. Bounce from 53% to under 40. Blog leads from 21 to 1,490-plus. And PMax spend review."

The close
💥

"We don't have a traffic problem. We have a page experience problem. The data is clear, the benchmarks are proven, and the plan is phased to validate before we invest. We're asking for alignment to start Phase 1 in May."

Phase 1: ~110 hours (web dev, UX, analytics). Phase 2: ~160 hours. Phase 3: ~170 hours. Total: ~440 hours over 3 months across 5 roles. Detailed project plan with RACI, dependencies, and risk register is ready to share.

We continue paying $8.9M/year in ad spend to drive traffic to pages that bounce 53% of visitors. Organic declining 40-50%, AI overviews accelerating it. Every month of delay = same budget, same conversion gap. The cost of inaction is measurable.
